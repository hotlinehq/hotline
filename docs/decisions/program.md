# Program decisions (Peter, 2026-10-28)

Full list in [docs/proxy-program.md](../proxy-program.md).

1. Commands `run`, `validate` and `version`. The config comes only from `--config`, with no default.
2. Own logs: JSON, one object per line, on standard error, with `--log-level`.
3. Stopping: up to 25 seconds (`--shutdown-timeout`); a second signal cuts it short.
4. Exit codes: 0 clean, 1 any failure (also a stop that ran out of time), 2 wrong command line.
5. `--trust-file` adds trusted certificate authorities on top of the system's own, so the end-to-end tests can trust their test vendor on any computer. This changed the spec's vendor certificate rule.
6. End-to-end tests use only the Go client for now. They run with `make e2e`, and `make test` adds their coverage to the 100% check.
7. The loaded config offers one `Integration` per ID, with the not-passed entry's `urlFull` and `content` filled in where the integration says nothing, wherever the entry is in the file. A call looks its integration up once (`Config.Integration`) and keeps it; the separate lookups for apis, trace, urlFull, content and the ledger chain are gone. The YAML is unchanged (Peter, 2026-11-16, from the decomplect review).
8. A stop lets long connections finish, the HAProxy way (Peter, 2026-11-23, Q1 and Q2 in the project files' `websocket-drop/requirements.md`). WebSockets, streamed responses and relayed tunnels open at the stop keep passing bytes both ways until they end on their own; four fifths into the time left once the drain delay ends (with the defaults, 21 of 25 seconds, leaving 4 to send the records), those still open, ordinary calls too, are cut and recorded in the time left, as HAProxy's `hard-stop-after` and Envoy's drain time do. Before, a stop cut them at once, so bytes already on their way could reach neither the vendor nor the message reader, which made a WebSocket scenario's coverage depend on timing. The proxy sends no close messages of its own and does not wait for a message to end, so a message still being sent at the cut is cut in half and gets no message record. Ingress is unchanged: new calls for the drain delay, then no new connections. Revisit if cut messages at the time limit turn out to matter: then stop between messages where the proxy reads them.
9. A signal that lives as long as the proxy, such as the stop's cut or "no more tunnels", is a channel closed once (`stopEvent` in `stopevent.go`), never a context kept in a struct (Peter, 2026-11-24, Q3). Go's guidance (the Go blog's "Contexts and structs") keeps contexts out of structs, and `containedctx` flags them; a context is made from the event only where a call takes one, such as a TLS handshake or a dial. Each wait on one costs one goroutine, where `context.AfterFunc` needed none. Revisit if that goroutine per call shows in a profile.
